Literary Devices Comparison

Texts: Six of Crows by Leigh Bardugo and The Cask of Amontillado by Edgar Allan Poe
Shared literary effect: Suspense

1. Six of Crows — Foreshadowing
Evidence (paraphrase): Kaz Brekker assembles a crew for an extremely dangerous mission, and early warnings about the Ice Court suggest the plan could fail.
Explanation: The warnings foreshadow obstacles the crew will face and create suspense about whether they will survive and succeed.
Page number: Requires the edition and an exact passage from the book.

2. The Cask of Amontillado — Symbolism
Evidence: “The man wore motley.”
Explanation: Fortunato's jester clothing symbolizes his foolishness and inability to recognize Montresor's deception. The reader's awareness of the danger adds suspense.
Page number: Depends on the textbook edition; near the beginning.

Connection: Both authors create suspense by emphasizing danger and uncertainty. Bardugo uses foreshadowing to suggest the risks ahead, while Poe uses symbolism to highlight Fortunato's vulnerability.

Note: The Six of Crows evidence above is a paraphrase, not a direct quotation. Exact quotation and page numbers should be verified against the student's edition.
